# Campaign seed: The Wending Vale (144x60)

DM-only. The heroes never read this; reveal it through narrate, npc_say, hooks, staged scenes and marks. One river, two banks, three crossings: the story is about who holds the water, and what holding it costs. Everything starts on the Fellowship's road. Townsfolk names are rolled; roles are canon. Tone: grounded, a little grit, dry humour. The river is not evil and nobody is simply wrong; every faction is half right and late. Death is permanent, so let a warning be fair before a danger is real.

## Premise
- **The River Is Held**: Long ago a weir was built where the Wend is wadeable, and a warden sworn to keep it. The river has run true ever since. The warden's oath is wearing thin, the river has begun to wander in its bed, and both banks blame the other. tags: premise, river, weir
- **Two Banks, One Water**: The Concord on the north bank and the Warband on the south each pay a toll to the old weir-keepers without knowing the other does. Whoever holds the three crossings holds the river's trade, and so far nobody has held them long. tags: premise, crossings, factions
- **A Vale That Runs Downhill**: The geography is the plot. The Wend rises at Kragmaw's lair in Rimecrown, passes the High Bridge, the Weirgate ford and the Low Bridge, and leaves by the sea past Gullstrand and Brandhold. Whatever goes wrong at the head reaches the mouth last, and the mouth is where everyone lives. Run the story upstream: early levels see the symptoms, late levels meet the cause. tags: premise, geography, river
- **What The Fellowship Is**: Five strangers with a hall in a home town and a river-letter to carry. The vale is short of people willing to cross a bank for another bank's sake, and that is exactly what it needs. tags: premise, fellowship

## Threads
- **The Wandering Bed**: Fishers at Gullstrand and Brandhold both report the Wend running a hand's breadth off its old line each morning. Mark a drowned post on the bank that was dry last week. Each week the line moves further, always toward the south bank first, then back, as if the river were searching. tags: wend, gullstrand, act1
- **The Weirgate Toll**: Travellers at the ford say a keeper in a green cloak asked a coin of them and gave a receipt written in a hand nobody can read. The receipts are real: stamped with a weir mark older than either faction. tags: weirgate, ford, act1
- **Greymane's Season**: The Pack-Father of the Hawkwold follows the herders' flocks and has begun to leave one lamb at each fold untouched. Herders at Hawkstead call it a tithe and have stopped hunting him; a hot-headed few want the bounty anyway. tags: greymane, elite, act1
- **The Smugglers' Lamp**: Lights move in the Sallowfen reeds at night, and the Sallow Vaults are said to be open again. A salt-run that never reaches Stiltmere tests the south bank. Whoever is copying the toll receipts is selling them, and a forged receipt will pass at the ford. tags: sallow, smugglers, act2
- **The High Bridge Quarrel**: Both factions have built a watch-tower within sight of the High Bridge and neither admits it. Kilnmark Cross changes hands. A shot fired by either tower is the war; keep a rumour of a third party ready. tags: highbridge, outposts, act2
- **The Falconer's Map**: Hawkstead's falconers still tell of Lord Merrin Hawkwright, who flew his birds over the Wend and kept a tally of its drift. His map of the old bed lies in the Kestrel Barrow, and it shows the river was never meant to run quite where it does. tags: kestrel, merrin, act1
- **Thornback in the Ford Wood**: The Boar-King of the Greywood has taken to rooting up the road to the ford, and the toll-keeper in green has not been seen on the days he is loose. Coincidence, or the keeper keeps clear of him. tags: thornback, greywood, elite, act2
- **The Dry Mouth**: Brandhold's smiths say the Cold Forge went cold the same week the river shifted, and Slagmaster Brok blames the water. The Brandwright Kiln, far upriver, is the only fire older than the Cold Forge and it has never gone out. tags: brandhold, kiln, act2
- **Ferry-Priest's Dawn**: Sister Hale blesses the Low Bridge every dawn. One morning the blessing is answered, once, by the wrong voice from under the arch. tags: hale, lowbridge, act2

## Characters
- **Gate Captain of Aldermere**: Concord gate post in the north-west, careful and dry. Wants the Fellowship to carry a river-letter to Hawkstead. Secret: her family kept a weir-key and she lost it. tags: aldermere, npc
- **Gate Warden of Brandhold**: Warband gate post in the south-west, blunt, pit-raised. Wants the south bank's iron to reach the weir before the river shifts. Secret: he has been paying the toll in iron for years. tags: brandhold, npc
- **Orsk, the Weir-Warden**: Sworn to hold the river, long dead, still holding it. Not a villain: a keeper whose oath is failing. Voice: slow, formal, counts everything. Wants a successor, or release. He will not lie, and he will not hurry; offer parley before blades if the company has earned a hearing. tags: orsk, weir, elite
- **Sister Hale, Ferry-Priest**: Crosses at the Low Bridge every dawn blessing the water. Wants a company to go to the head of the river. Secret: she has seen the spring run backward. tags: priest, ally
- **Gate Captain of Gullstrand**: Concord harbour post, salt-hoarse, tired of fish politics. Wants proof the river is moving before the harbour dues are cut. Secret: he has been moving the harbour posts himself each morning to hide how far the water has gone. tags: gullstrand, npc
- **Gate Captain of Hawkstead**: Concord upland market, keeps a falcon on the gate-post and knows every herder by name. Wants the Greymane matter settled without a hunt. Secret: she has promised the herders that no bounty will be posted. tags: hawkstead, npc
- **Stiltmere's Reedmaster**: Warband fen town, speaks softly and counts his stilts. Wants the Sallow smugglers stopped, quietly. Secret: his own cousin runs the salt. tags: stiltmere, npc
- **Hornridge's Bonewarden**: Warband ridge-town on the south bank, road to the ford. Wants the High Bridge tower manned before the Concord's is. Secret: he has begun to doubt the war is worth the horn. tags: hornridge, npc
- **Lord Merrin Hawkwright**: Falconer-lord of the Kestrel Barrow, long dead, bound to his mews. Cares about birds, the river and being right about both. Wants his map read by someone who will act on it. tags: merrin, kestrel, elite
- **Captain Dane Sallow**: Smuggler-captain of the Sallow Vaults. Charming, a little vain, counts every barrel. Wants the road open and the receipts selling. Secret: his forged receipts are better than the keeper's own, and someone has noticed. tags: dane, sallow, elite
- **The Kiln-Warden**: A golem built to tend the Brandwright Kiln, still tending it. Voice: few words, all of them instructions. Wants fuel and to be told when to stop. tags: kiln, warden, elite
- **The Green Keeper**: The cloaked toll-collector at the ford, seen only by day and never twice in one spot. Wants a coin and gives a receipt. Not a person: the weir's own hand, a remnant of Orsk's oath that walks the crossings. It does not argue and it cannot be bribed past the toll. tags: green-keeper, weir, mystery
- **First-delve keepers**: Miller Quill (Aldermere), Old Brine (Gullstrand), Shepherd Wick (Hawkstead), Slagmaster Brok (Brandhold), Boil-Master Garr (Stiltmere), Mother Rattle (Hornridge). Each is a small, human trouble with one object that points up the river; let the company's own hall decide which it meets. tags: delves, npc

## Plans
- **Act I, levels 1-5, Wendmouth**: The wandering bed, the first toll, a gate captain's letter. Keep it small: two towns' worth of road and a first delve. Teach the vale's rule: every bank has a story, and the river is in both. tags: act1
- **Act II, levels 6-11, Sallowfen, Hawkwold, Greywood, the Drowned Weir**: The two banks meet at the ford. The Weir holds the oath and Orsk's chair; the High Bridge is where factions clash. Both sides learn the toll was one toll. Let the company be offered the Weir's chair as a bargaining piece before they know what it is. tags: act2
- **Act III, levels 12-20, Ashenfold, Rimecrown**: The Kiln-Warden at the Brandwright Kiln, and Kragmaw at the spring, whose heat is what has been drying the river's head. Choose: free the warden, relieve him, or let the river go where it will. tags: act3
- **Opening beats, days 1-4**: Day 1: the first delve and the gate writ. Day 2: a fisher with a drowned post. Day 3: Sister Hale at the Low Bridge asks about the spring. Day 4: a green-cloaked toll-keeper at a ford rumour. If a member falls, the oath passes to the replacement. tags: openings
- **The three crossings as a clock**: The Low Bridge is the first to feel the shift (days). The Weirgate ford is where the toll is paid (weeks). The High Bridge is where the war starts (months). Let each crossing's trouble arrive in that order and never together, so the company always has one to answer. tags: crossings, pacing
- **The four dungeons as four answers**: The Kestrel Barrow shows the river as it was. The Sallow Vaults show who profits from its failing. The Drowned Weir holds the oath that keeps it. The Brandwright Kiln holds the fire that could mend the weir or finish it. Do not run them in a fixed order; let the company's questions choose. tags: dungeons, pacing
- **Faction dials**: Concord and Warband patrols start at wary and move toward open quarrel by one step for each unanswered High Bridge incident. They step back one when a company is seen carrying a letter or a toll across the river without taking sides. Never let either faction become the villain; let both have a gate captain who would rather not. tags: factions, pacing
- **If the company falls**: Death is permanent. A replacement takes the fallen member's place at the next camp or town and inherits their oath; the river does not wait, but let one thread rest for a day. tags: hardcore

## Prologue
- **The first delve**: The company's first dungeon is a small one near its hall: read `first_delve` in dm_status for the place. Keep it small and human, a chore that turns out to be real. Hint at the river's trouble through one object below: a wet footprint, a toll receipt in an unreadable hand.

## Secrets
- **The Toll Is One Toll**: Both banks have paid the same unseen keeper for generations; the receipts are in the Weir-Warden's chair. Where: Orsk himself, or a Lore roll at the Weirgate Watch. tags: toll, reveal
- **Kragmaw Dries the Spring**: The wyrm's heat has drawn the headwaters back a mile a decade. Where: Sister Hale's sight of the spring, or the lair's cold trail. tags: kragmaw, spring
- **The Green Keeper Is The Oath**: The cloaked collector is not a man; it is what is left of Orsk's oath walking the crossings. As the oath thins it appears less, collects less and the river wanders more. Where: Orsk's chair, or a receipt held to lamplight at the Weirgate Watch. tags: green-keeper, orsk, reveal
- **The Weir Wants Stone From The Kiln**: The weir was built of green stone fired at the Brandwright Kiln, and only that fire can mend it. The Kiln-Warden still holds the last of the stone. Where: the Kiln Heart, or Lord Merrin's map. tags: kiln, weir, reveal
- **Merrin's Map Shows The Old Bed**: The river was moved, deliberately, centuries ago, and the weir was built to hold it in the new bed. It is not wandering: it is going home. Where: the Kestrel Niches in the Kestrel Barrow. tags: merrin, map, reveal
- **The Forged Receipts**: Captain Dane Sallow's forgeries pass the ford's keeper better than honest coin, and he has learned how the oath is paid: one coin a crossing, forever. He plans to sell the knowledge to the Concord. Where: the Salt Tally in the Sallow Vaults. tags: dane, forgery, reveal
- **The Gullstrand Harbour Posts**: The harbour captain has been moving his marker posts each night. The river's true line, taken from the drowned post on the bank, shows how far it has gone. Where: a night watch at the harbour, or the captain's own confession. tags: gullstrand, posts, reveal
- **Three Endings**: Free the warden (Orsk released, the weir unheld, the Wend goes where it will); relieve him (a successor takes the oath, perhaps a company member who accepts the chair); or mend the weir with kiln-stone and hold the old river in the new bed again. None is clean: freeing floods Gullstrand, relieving costs a life's freedom, mending needs Kragmaw dead or the spring dies. Let the company choose, and let the chronicle remember. tags: endings, act3
